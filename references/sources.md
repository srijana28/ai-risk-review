# Key References

This project is informed by widely cited research and policy frameworks on AI safety, large language model (LLM) risks, and AI governance.

---

## Hallucination & Reliability

1. Ji et al. (2023)  
   **Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation**  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05232  
   - Comprehensive overview of hallucination types, causes, and evaluation methods.

2. OpenAI (2023)  
   **GPT-4 System Card**  
   https://cdn.openai.com/papers/gpt-4-system-card.pdf  
   - Describes real-world risks, hallucinations, and mitigation strategies in frontier models.

---

## Bias & Social Harms

3. Bender et al. (2021)  
   **On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots**  
   https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3442188.3445922  
   - Foundational paper on bias, scale, and societal risks of large language models.

---

## Misuse & Safety Risks

4. Weidinger et al. (2021)  
   **Ethical and Social Risks of Harm from Language Models**  
   https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3442188.3445922  
   - Introduces a structured taxonomy of LLM risks, including misinformation and misuse.

5. Shevlane et al. (2023)  
   **Model Evaluation for Extreme Risks**  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15324  
   - Proposes evaluation methods for severe and misuse-related AI risks.

---

## AI Governance & Policy Frameworks

6. NIST (2023)  
   **AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)**  
   https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework  
   - Practical framework for identifying, assessing, and mitigating AI risks.

7. OECD (2019)  
   **OECD Principles on Artificial Intelligence**  
   https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles  
   - International principles for trustworthy and responsible AI.

---

## Notes
- This list focuses on high-impact, policy-relevant sources.
- References are used to support qualitative risk analysis and governance recommendations.
