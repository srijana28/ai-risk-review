# AI Risk Review
Evaluating risks of Large Language Models (LLMs) and proposing mitigation strategies from an engineering and policy perspective.

## Overview
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used in high-impact domains such as healthcare, education, governance, and information access. While powerful, these systems can introduce risks including hallucinations, misinformation, bias, and unsafe outputs.

This project conducts a structured risk review of two widely used LLMs:
- ChatGPT
- Gemini

The goal is to evaluate real-world harms and propose actionable mitigation strategies that combine technical safeguards and AI governance approaches.

## Models Evaluated
- ChatGPT (manual testing)
- Gemini (manual testing)

## Risk Categories
- Hallucinations
- Misinformation
- Bias & Stereotyping
- Unsafe or Harmful Instructions

## Methodology
- A curated prompt suite was designed for each risk category.
- Model outputs were evaluated using a standardized risk rubric.
- Results were logged and analyzed qualitatively.
- Mitigation strategies were proposed based on findings.

## Key Questions
- How often do LLMs hallucinate or provide misleading information?
- Do models reinforce social or gender bias?
- How well do models refuse unsafe or harmful requests?
- What safeguards are currently effective, and where do gaps remain?

## Deliverables
- Prompt suite
- Risk evaluation rubric
- Test results and analysis
- Mitigation strategies (technical + policy)
- A short policy brief with recommendations

## Why This Matters
As LLMs scale in capability and adoption, systematic risk evaluation and governance become critical. This project aims to bridge technical evaluation with policy-relevant insights.

## Notes & Limitations

- This evaluation is based on manual testing and a limited prompt set.
- Results are qualitative and illustrative rather than statistically exhaustive.
- Model behavior may change over time due to updates and safety tuning.
- Harmful content is summarized rather than reproduced to avoid amplification.

Despite these limitations, the findings highlight recurring risk patterns relevant for real-world AI deployment and policy discussion.
